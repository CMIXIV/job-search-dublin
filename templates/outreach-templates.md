# Outreach templates

Drafts, not sends. Adapt to the person's voice - if they would never write "I
hope this finds you well", it does not go in. Short beats polished.

## Telling your network

> Hi all - [company] announced redundancies this week and my role is affected.
> I'm looking for [target title] roles in [location], ideally in [domain]. The
> short version of what I do: [one line, with the number].
>
> If you hear of anything, or know someone worth talking to, I'd be grateful. CV
> attached. Happy to return the favour whenever it's useful.

No apology, no self-deprecation, no long explanation. A redundancy is a business
event and most people already know.

## Asking for a referral

> Hi [name] - I saw [company] is hiring a [role]. I'm looking after [company]
> announced redundancies, and it looks like a real fit for the [specific thing] I
> did at [employer] - [one concrete outcome with a number].
>
> Would you be willing to flag my application internally, or point me to whoever
> is hiring? Completely fine if not. CV attached either way.

Send it *before or alongside* the application, not two weeks after.

## Reconnecting with a dormant contact

> Hi [name] - it's been a while since [specific shared thing]. Hope [company] is
> treating you well.
>
> I'm on the market after redundancies at [company], looking at [target titles] in
> [location]. Not asking you to solve anything - mostly just letting people know,
> and if anything crosses your desk I'd appreciate the heads up.
>
> Would be good to catch up regardless.

## LinkedIn invitation - the 200-character version

A LinkedIn invitation note is capped at **200 characters**, and the cap is
enforced silently at the keyboard: a longer note simply will not send. Count it,
do not estimate it - `python3 -c "print(len(open('note.txt').read().strip()))"` -
and put the count in the heading of whatever file the draft lives in.

Anything that needs more than 200 characters is two messages. Version A buys the
connection; version B carries the substance once it is accepted.

**Version A - the invitation (must fit 200).** Warm signal, the application named
once, one credential, an ask that costs nothing.

> Hi [name] - applied for the [role] this week. Spent [N] years doing [the one
> thing] at [employer]. Would value being connected either way.

**Version B - after they accept (no limit).**

> Thanks for connecting. I applied for the [role] on [date] - the part that stood
> out was [specific thing from the JD], and the closest thing I've done is [one
> outcome, one number].
>
> If it's useful I can send a short note on how I'd approach [the specific
> thing]. And if it's not the right fit, I'd still welcome the connection.

Worth checking on LinkedIn's own help pages, not from memory: free accounts may
also cap *personalised* invitations per month. If they do, spend the notes on the
warmest targets rather than the most overdue ones.

## Cold outreach to a hiring manager

Use after applying, when the manager can be identified and the role matters.

> Hi [name] - I applied for the [role] on your team this week. Rather than leave
> it in the queue: the part of the job that stood out was [specific thing from the
> JD], and the closest thing I've done is [one outcome, one number].
>
> If it's useful I'm happy to send a short note on how I'd approach [the specific
> thing]. If it's not a fit, no reply needed.

One message. No follow-up sequence. This is not sales, and treating it like sales
is how people get remembered badly.

## Following up on an application

Ten to fourteen days after applying, once.

> Hi [name] - following up on my application for [role], submitted [date]. Still
> very interested; happy to provide anything that would help. Thanks for taking a
> look.

## After a screen

Within 24 hours.

> Thanks for the time today. [Specific thing from the conversation] was the part I
> found most interesting - [one sentence of substance about it].
>
> Happy to expand on [thing they probed] if useful. Looking forward to hearing
> where things go.

## After a final round

> Thanks again for the conversation on [date]. One thing I've kept thinking about:
> [the problem they described]. If I were in the seat, the first 90 days would go
> at [one concrete approach].
>
> Whatever you decide, I appreciated the process.

## Reply to a recruiter you spoke to months ago

> Hi [name] - we spoke about [role] back in [month]. Circumstances have changed:
> [company] announced redundancies and I'm now actively looking, at [target
> titles] in [location].
>
> Is anything live that would fit? Updated CV attached.

## After a rejection

> Thanks for letting me know - appreciate you closing the loop, which not everyone
> does.
>
> If there's anything specific that made the difference, I'd genuinely welcome it -
> and if something else opens up on the team, I'd be glad to hear from you.

Most will not give feedback. Ask once, stay warm, and keep the door open - the
role that was filled sometimes reopens in three months.
