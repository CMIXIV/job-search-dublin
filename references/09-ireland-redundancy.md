# Ireland: redundancy, notice, tax and welfare

Pull this whenever money, notice, consultation or "should I take the package"
comes up. It changes the runway figure in phase 2, and runway sets the whole
search.

**This is orientation, not advice.** You are not a solicitor, a tax adviser or a
financial adviser, and you should say so once, plainly, without hedging every
sentence afterwards. Figures were verified in August 2026 and change - the
statutory ceiling and welfare rates move with the Budget. Point the person at
`citizensinformation.ie`, `revenue.ie` and `workplacerelations.ie` for the
current position, and tell them to get proper advice before signing anything.

## Statutory redundancy

Requires **104 weeks (2 years) of continuous service** with the employer.

The formula is **2 weeks' gross pay per complete year of service, plus one bonus
week**, with gross weekly pay **capped at EUR 600** for the calculation. Only
complete years count - 9 years and 11 months is 9 years.

Statutory redundancy is **exempt from income tax, USC and PRSI**.

The cap matters and is frequently misunderstood: someone earning EUR 90,000 is
calculated at EUR 600 a week, not at their actual salary. Do the arithmetic
explicitly for the person rather than letting them assume.

If the employer cannot pay, the payment can be claimed from the Social Insurance
Fund. The employer files form RP50.

## Minimum notice

Statutory minimum notice by length of continuous service:

| Service | Notice |
|---|---|
| 13 weeks to 2 years | 1 week |
| 2 to 5 years | 2 weeks |
| 5 to 10 years | 4 weeks |
| 10 to 15 years | 6 weeks |
| 15 years or more | 8 weeks |

The **contract may give more** - check it, because contractual notice frequently
exceeds the statutory minimum and is worth real money.

**PILON versus worked notice versus garden leave** are materially different:
whether they are paid in a lump or over time, whether benefits and pension
continue, whether they can start a new job during the period, and how it is
taxed. If a package offers a choice, work through each option's cash and timing
with them - and note that a preference expressed as a collective position in
consultation may constrain what an individual can later negotiate.

During the final two weeks of notice, employees are entitled to reasonable paid
time off to look for work or arrange training.

## Collective redundancy

Where the number of proposed redundancies passes thresholds that scale with the
size of the establishment, collective redundancy rules under the Protection of
Employment Act 1977 apply:

- A **minimum 30-day consultation period** with employee representatives, begun at
  the earliest opportunity and at least 30 days before the first notice of
  dismissal is given
- Consultation must be **"with a view to reaching agreement"**, and the employer
  must supply all relevant information to the representatives
- The **Minister for Enterprise must be notified**, and no dismissal may take
  effect until at least 30 days after that notification
- No notice of redundancy may be issued during the information and consultation
  process

Practical implication for the person: the consultation window is a negotiating
window, and it is finite. Selection criteria must be objective and applied
fairly; suitable alternative employment must be considered.

## Tax on an ex-gratia (above-statutory) payment

Anything above statutory redundancy is taxable, reduced by whichever **one** of
these reliefs is largest:

- **Basic Exemption** - EUR 10,160 plus EUR 765 for each complete year of service
- **Increased Exemption** - Basic Exemption plus EUR 10,000, available only if no
  tax-free termination payment was claimed in the previous 10 tax years and they
  either are not in an occupational pension scheme or irrevocably give up the
  right to a lump sum from it
- **SCSB** - (average annual pay over the last 36 months x complete years of
  service / 15) minus any tax-free pension lump sum already received. Usually the
  largest for higher earners and long service

There is a **EUR 200,000 lifetime cap** on tax-free ex-gratia payments.

Work out all three and take the highest. The difference between the Basic
Exemption and SCSB for someone with fifteen years' service is often five figures,
which is why "what's the package worth after tax" is a real question and not a
rhetorical one.

## Welfare while searching

**Jobseeker's Benefit** - maximum personal rate **EUR 254 per week** as of January
2026, with lower graduated rates for lower previous earnings.

**Jobseeker's Pay-Related Benefit** - for those with at least 5 years of paid PRSI
contributions: **60% of previous earnings (max EUR 450/week) for 13 weeks**, then
**55% (max EUR 375) for 13 weeks**, then **50% (max EUR 300) for 13 weeks**.

Apply promptly - payment runs from the application, and the redundancy payment
itself does not disqualify them. This is often worth more than people assume and
it belongs in the runway calculation.

## Employment permit holders

If the person holds a Critical Skills or General Employment Permit tied to the
employer making them redundant, this is the **most urgent item in their entire
search** and outranks everything else in this skill.

There is a defined period after redundancy in which a new employment permit must
be secured, and there are notification requirements. The specifics change - have
them confirm the current position directly with the Department of Enterprise,
Tourism and Employment immediately, and treat the deadline as the hard constraint
in phase 2.

**How the package is structured can break an immigration application.** This
catches people and it is worth raising unprompted:

- **PILON ends employment immediately.** Any permission, scheme or application
  that requires the person to be *currently employed* fails the moment the
  employment ends. Worked notice or garden leave keeps them employed for the full
  notice period. Where an immigration route depends on current employment, that
  is a second and entirely independent reason to prefer notice over PILON, and it
  belongs in the negotiation.
- **Continuity requirements are ambiguous.** A criterion reading "in employment
  for six months" may mean six months of continuous employment generally, or six
  months with the current employer. The difference can be half a year of
  eligibility. Verify with a specialist before accepting any offer whose start
  date sits inside the window - not afterwards.
- **Contractor engagements are a different status.** Self-employment is accepted
  by some routes, but switching from employment to self-employment mid-window is
  often untested. Ask before signing.

Where an immigration route is in play, everything else in the search is
downstream of holding it. Treat it as the first constraint, not a side issue.

## Recruitment agencies: consent before submission

This one protects the person and it is routinely not known, so raise it before
they deal with any agency.

**Insist on consent for a named employer before any CV goes out.** The firm
ground here is data protection: a CV is personal data, and under GDPR the person
is entitled to know who it is being shared with, to object, and to withdraw. "I
have a great role, send me your CV" is not consent to submit anywhere, and the
person is entitled to say so plainly.

Do not rely on industry self-regulation to enforce this. The National Recruitment
Federation operates a code of conduct for its members, but membership is
voluntary and many agencies are not members - so treat the code as a reason to
prefer an NRF agency, not as a protection that applies by default. The protection
that always applies is the person saying no, in writing, before sending anything.

The script is short and not confrontational:

> Happy to send my CV. Before you submit it anywhere, tell me the company name
> and confirm you will not send it on without my say-so for each specific role.

Why it matters concretely for someone in a redundancy process:

- **Their own employer.** A blind submission can land their CV back at the company
  making them redundant, or at a client of it. Deeply awkward while still on
  payroll and mid-consultation.
- **Duplicate submissions.** If the person already applied direct, or another
  agency has submitted them, employers commonly reject both candidates rather than
  arbitrate the fee - losing a role they would otherwise have had.
- **Companies they have ruled out.** The culture and depth filters from
  `07-fit-scoring.md` only work if the person controls where they are put forward.

Also worth knowing: it is **illegal to charge a jobseeker a fee** simply for
agreeing to find them work - the employer pays. Anyone asking the person for money
to place them is not operating legitimately. Employment agencies operating in
Ireland must hold a licence, administered through the Workplace Relations
Commission's licensing section, so a licence can be checked before engaging.

If an agency submits them somewhere without consent, that is a data-protection
complaint they can raise with the Data Protection Commission - and with the NRF
too if the agency happens to be a member. Say it once; do not turn it into a
campaign while they are mid-search.

## Should they take the package

Not your call, and say so. What you can do is lay out the comparison:

- Total value after tax, using the right relief
- Notice treatment and whether they can earn elsewhere during it
- Pension, health cover, unvested equity, and what happens to each
- Any non-compete, non-solicit or garden leave restriction on their next role -
  read the actual clause rather than assuming, check whether the specific target
  employers fall inside it, and have a solicitor confirm before signing anything
- The realistic time-to-hire in their band and market, from their own tracker
  data if they have any, and from recruiters if they do not

Then note the two things people systematically get wrong: they underestimate the
after-tax value of the package because they forget the statutory portion is
tax-free, and they underestimate time-to-hire at senior levels. Both errors push
in the same direction.

If real money or a contested selection is involved, recommend a solicitor. In a
collective process, the union or employee representatives may have arranged
advice - worth asking before paying for it independently.
