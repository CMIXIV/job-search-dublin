# Phase 2 - Priorities and target titles

Goal: a small, specific target that the person's evidence can actually win, with
their real constraints written down.

Output: `job-search/02-priorities.md`.

## Ask these, in this order

**1. Runway, in months.** Not "urgent" or "no rush". Money: statutory and
ex-gratia payment expected, notice or PILON, savings, partner income, welfare
entitlement, mortgage or visa deadlines. In Ireland, pull
`09-ireland-redundancy.md` and compute it properly - most people underestimate
their runway because they forget the payment is largely tax-free, and some
overestimate it because they forget tax on the ex-gratia portion.

Runway sets everything downstream. Three months and twelve months are different
searches, not the same search at different speeds.

**2. Employment type.** Permanent, contract, fractional, consulting, interim,
part-time. Ask whether contract is acceptable *as a bridge* even if permanent is
preferred - it widens the funnel materially and many people rule it out by
default without deciding to.

**3. Visa and work authorisation.** Whether they need sponsorship, whether a
permit is tied to the employer they are leaving, and any deadline attached to it.
This is a hard filter on every role and it is easier to ask now than to discover
in week six. In Ireland: a Critical Skills or General Employment Permit holder
being made redundant has a defined period to find a new employer - tell them to
confirm the current period with the Department of Enterprise, since it changes.

**4. Location and work mode.** City, commute tolerance, hybrid days, fully remote,
open to relocation. Then the honest follow-up: "remote" in a job ad frequently
means "remote within one country for tax reasons" - so record which countries
they can legally be employed in, not just where they would like to sit.

**5. Compensation floor and target.** Two numbers: what they will not go below,
and what they are aiming at. If they do not know the market, look it up before
they anchor themselves low - it is the single most common self-inflicted wound in
a redundancy search.

**6. Target titles.** Ask for three to five. Then challenge them.

**7. Non-negotiables and deal-breakers.** Industries they will not work in,
commute limits, manager-of-managers vs individual contributor, on-call, travel.

## Challenge the titles - in both directions

Compare the target titles against the scope ceiling from phase 1. There are three
cases and each needs a different conversation.

**Aiming above the evidence.** They want Principal, their largest evidenced scope
reads Senior. Say it directly, with the specific gap: "Principal roles here are
screening on org-wide influence and multi-team ownership. Your strongest evidence
is deep ownership of one large platform. That's a Senior-plus story." Then give
them the choice with real numbers: apply at both bands and accept a lower hit
rate at the higher one, or target the band they can win and get in fast.

**Aiming below the evidence.** More common than people expect after a redundancy,
because confidence takes the hit. If someone with 15 years and P&L responsibility
is targeting mid-level roles, say so - they will be screened out as overqualified
and will resent the job if they get it.

**Aiming sideways into a new domain.** Legitimate, but the transferable story has
to be explicit and the hit rate will be lower for the first month. Ask what
evidence bridges the gap, and if there is none, ask what would create some in
four weeks.

**Then check the titles exist.** The same job is called different things by
different companies. Search the target sources for each title and count live
postings. A title with two open roles in the whole city is a hobby, not a target.
Note the synonyms that show up - they belong in the saved searches in phase 4.

## Write it down

`job-search/02-priorities.md`:

- Runway in months, with the arithmetic shown
- Target titles, ranked, with live-posting counts and synonyms
- Titles considered and rejected, with the reason (stops the same debate in week
  six)
- Employment types accepted, including bridges
- Locations, work modes, countries of legal employment
- Compensation floor and target, with the market data used
- Hard filters: visa, notice, industries, travel
- Search intensity implied by the runway: applications per week, and what gets
  dropped if that is not being hit

Re-read this at every monthly review. Runway shortens; priorities that were
optional in month one become decisions in month three.
