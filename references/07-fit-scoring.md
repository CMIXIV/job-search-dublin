# Phase 7a - Fit scoring

Every role gets a number from 1 to 10 and one line of reasoning. The point is not
precision; it is a consistent basis for deciding where a limited number of
tailoring hours go.

## Gates first - these are not scored

If a role fails one of these, it is out regardless of how good it looks. Say
which gate and move on.

- **Work authorisation** - can they legally be employed there, without
  sponsorship the employer will not provide
- **Location** - "remote" often means remote-within-one-country for tax reasons.
  Check the practicals section before spending an hour on the application
- **Compensation floor** - if a band is published and it is below the floor from
  phase 2, it is out. If no band is published, it is not a gate, it is a question
  for the screen
- **Deal-breakers** from phase 2 - industry, travel, on-call, management scope

## Then score, out of 10

Six dimensions. Weights are a starting point; adjust for the person and say so.

**Seniority band match - 25%.** Does their evidenced scope sit at, just below, or
above this role's band? At band scores high; one band up is a stretch worth a
point or two off; two bands up is a lottery ticket and should be scored like one.

**Core requirement match - 25%.** Take the three things the job description
repeats - repetition is the tell for what the hiring manager actually cares
about, more reliable than the ordering of the bullet list. Can the person point
to concrete evidence for each? Three of three is a 10 here. One of three is a 3.

**Domain and context - 15%.** Industry, company stage, B2B vs B2C, regulated vs
not, scale. Adjacent counts for most of the credit. Entirely new is not
disqualifying but it lengthens the odds, and it should show in the number.

**Competition signals - 15%.** Posting age (a role live for six weeks is either
stalled or has a shortlist), Easy Apply versus a real form, applicant counts if
shown, agency listing versus direct, and whether the same role has been reposted.

**Advocate - 10%.** Does the person know anyone inside? A warm introduction moves
the odds more than any CV edit. This is the dimension most worth *creating* -
a 6 with an advocate beats an 8 without one.

**Enthusiasm - 10%.** Would they actually take it on a Tuesday in March? Ask
directly. Low enthusiasm shows up in the cover note, then in the screen, and a
search full of roles they do not want is how people burn out in month two.

## What the number means

- **9-10** - drop other work, apply this week, activate every advocate
- **7-8** - apply with full tailoring, worth two hours
- **5-6** - apply if the weekly capacity is not full. Prioritise those with an
  advocate or an unusually strong single dimension
- **3-4** - skip, unless there is an advocate or a specific reason. Log it with
  the reason so the decision is not re-litigated
- **1-2** - no

## Score twice, and be willing to go down

**Pass 1** on the listing card - title, company, band, location, summary. Cheap,
fast, filters the list.

**Pass 2** after reading the full job description on the company's own board.
This is where scores move, and they move down at least as often as up. A role
that reads as an AI product role in the summary can turn out to be mostly
compliance work once the responsibilities are listed.

State the revision explicitly: *"Reading the full JD lowered this from 8 to 7 -
the AI content is implied rather than stated, and the compliance domain is new."*
A score that only ever moves up is not a score.

## Record it

Tracker fields: fit score, one-line rationale, and - when it changes - the
revision and its reason. Three weeks later, when the person asks why they did not
apply to something, the answer is written down.

Save the JD text to `job-search/applications/<company>-<role>/jd.md` at the time
of scoring. Postings disappear, and interview prep needs the original.
